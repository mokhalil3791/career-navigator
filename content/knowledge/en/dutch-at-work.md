**Levels.** Language levels use the European scale **CEFR**: A1 and A2 (basic), B1 and B2 (independent), C1 and C2 (proficient).

**Staatsexamen NT2.** The state exam Dutch as a second language (staatsexamen Nt2) is an official exam for people aged 17 and older who want to work, study or become Dutch citizens. It has two programmes:

- **Programme I: level B1.** Recommended for study or work at vocational level (mbo).
- **Programme II: level B2.** Suitable for jobs in Dutch-speaking companies and for study at (applied) universities.

Each programme tests reading, writing, listening and speaking. If you pass all four, you receive the **Diploma staatsexamen Nt2**, which does not expire.

**Civic integration (inburgering)** is a separate process run by DUO, with its own exams. The inburgeren.nl website has information in several languages, including Arabic, Tigrinya, Dari, Persian and Turkish.

**Reading an advert.** Look carefully at how the advert talks about Dutch:

- **“Dutch required” (Nederlands vereist):** often with a level, for example “Dutch B2”.
- **“Dutch is a plus” (Nederlands is een pré):** helpful but not a must.
- **Not stated:** do not guess from the language the advert is written in. Ask the employer.

In **Compare jobs**, Career Navigator flags the Dutch-language demand separately, because it is often the deciding factor.

**On your CV (example, fictional):** “Dutch: B1 (Staatsexamen NT2 programme I, 2026)” or “Dutch: A2 (self-assessed)”. Write a level only if you can show it, and say whether it is certified or self-assessed.
