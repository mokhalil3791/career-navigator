**The parts of a CV.** werk.nl (the job site of UWV) lists six usual parts:

- **Personal details (persoonlijke gegevens):** name, address, email and phone number. Use a clear email address with your name in it. Add your LinkedIn link if you have one.
- **Personal profile (persoonlijk profiel or profielschets):** a few sentences about who you are, what you can do and what matters to you in work. Change it for each vacancy.
- **Work experience (werkervaring):** jobs and internships, with your **latest job at the top**. Describe your tasks and responsibilities briefly.
- **Education and courses (opleidingen en cursussen):** also newest first.
- **Side activities (nevenactiviteiten):** for example volunteering or coaching a sports team, if they show useful knowledge.
- **Skills and competences (vaardigheden en competenties):** for example computer programs and languages you speak.

werk.nl's list of personal details does not include a photo, date of birth, nationality or marital status. Career Navigator has no fields for them.

werk.nl also suggests saving your CV as a **PDF** so the layout stays the same, and naming the file with your name.

**Missing experience in the field?** werk.nl suggests a **skills-based CV (vaardigheden-cv)**, which puts competences, skills and results first.

**The letter (sollicitatiebrief).** werk.nl advises you to:

- start with a first sentence that makes the employer curious, or refer to a phone call you had;
- explain why you want to work at **this** organisation;
- show why you fit, using examples from earlier jobs;
- not repeat everything that is already on your CV;
- end positively and say you would like to explain your application in a conversation.

**Example opening (fictional):** “Thank you for our phone call last Thursday. Below are three reasons why I fit this role.”

In Career Navigator, **My application** builds the CV from your own recorded facts, and the review box makes sure you check every claim before you download.
